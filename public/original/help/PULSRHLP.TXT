**Radio Telescope Controls**
The following buttons are used to operate the radio telescope.  They are
located on the telescope control panel.  Depending on settings selected
by your instructor, slewing may be limited by the local horizon and
possibly by other limitations of the telescope.

    "Tracking" - This button turns on the sidereal tracking that enables
    the telescope to remain pointed at a particular spot on the sky as
    the Earth turns.  When tracking is off, celestial objects will drift
    through the telescope field, as indicated by the changing Right
    Ascension readout.  Tracking is off when the telescope is first
    accessed.  NOTE: if the telescope is a meridian transit instrument,
    the "Tracking" button will not appear on the control panel.

    "Slew Rate" - This pair of buttons are used to set the rate at which
    the telescope moves when a slew button (N,S,E,W) is pressed.  The
    rate can be varied by factors of ten through a fairly wide range.

    Slew Buttons ("N","S","E","W") - The slew buttons are used to move
    the telescope to point to a particular spot (direction) in the sky.
    To move the telescope, press and hold the appropriate button.  The
    telescope will slew at a speed determined by the slew rate setting.
    Release the button to stop the slew.  For long slews, the button
    will stay down if you slide the cursor off of it before releasing
    the mouse button.  In this case, stop the slew by pressing and
    releasing the mouse button with the cursor over the active slew
    button. NOTE: if the telescope is a meridian transit instrument the
    "E" and "W" buttons will not appear on the control panel.

    "Receiver" - Press this button to access the first or "master"
    receiver.

**Set Coordinates**
To enter the desired pointing coordinates for the telescope directly,
select "Slew"->"Set Coordinates..." from the radio telescope control
panel menu and enter the coordinates on the form that appears.  This is
generally faster than using the slew buttons to move the telescope from
object to object, unless the objects are very near each other in the
sky.  NOTE: if the telescope is a meridian transit instrument, this
facility will move the telescope in Declination only.

**Remarks on Slewing the Telescope**
The portion of the sky that is accessible to a telescope at any given
time is limited by several factors.  In reality, no telescope, optical
or radio, can "see" below the local horizon.  If so configured, the
telescope in this exercise may not slew below an altitude that is
several degrees above the "true" geographic horizon.

In addition, many radio telescopes are restricted by their design to
limited regions of the sky overhead.  The most common of these is the
meridian transit telescope, which can slew only in Declination, and must
wait for the object being observed to be carried through the field of
view by the rotation of the Earth.  Another type of restriction is
exemplified by the Arecibo 1000 ft. dish, which can be pointed only at
objects within a few degrees of the local zenith.  This software can be
set to simulate either of these slewing restrictions - your instructor
will inform you if this has been done.  If the software is set to
simulate a meridian transit instrument, the "Tracking", "E" and "W"
buttons will not appear on the control panel, and the "Set Coordinates"
and "Hot List" facilities will slew the telescope in Declination only.

Unlike optical telescopes, radio telescopes are usable in either
daylight or darkness, so the easiest solution to observing objects that
are currently out of the accessible range of the telescope (in Right
Ascension) is to wait a few hours, or with this software, change the
simulated date/time.

In order to simplify this exercise, your instructor may set the software
to ignore all slewing restrictions and slew to any part of the sky at
any time.  You will be informed if this is the case.

See also;
    "Set the Observation Date & Time"

**Select a "Hot List" Object**
To slew the telescope to an item on the Hot List, select "Slew"->"Hot
List"-> "View/Select from List..." from the radio telescope control
panel menu.  When the list appears, click on the desired entry to
highlight it.  Press "OK" and the telescope will slew to the object.
(The software may be configured to allow slewing only to objects that
are above the instrument's horizon and within its slewing range.)  If
the telescope is a meridian transit instrument, the "Hot List" facility
will move the telescope in Declination only.

See also;
    "Remarks on Slewing the Telescope"

**Add an Object to the "Hot List"**
You can add interesting objects to the Hot List to make it easy to
return to them later for additional observation.  To add an object,
accurately point the telescope toward it and access a receiver to verify
that you have located a pulsar.  Next, select "Slew"->"Hot List" ->"Add
Current Item to List..." from the radio telescope control panel menu. In
the form that appears, you can add a comment to help you identify the
object or why it is of interest.  (You can also change the
identification and position to add an object that is not currently in
the telescope field.) When you have completed the entry, press "OK" to
add it to the list. (If you create a bad entry by mistake, see the Help
Topic "Remove an Object from the "Hot List".)

**Remove an Object from the "Hot List"**
To remove an entry from the Hot List, select "Slew:->"Hot List"->
"View/Select from List..." from the radio telescope control panel menu.
When the list appears, click on the desired entry to highlight it, then
press the "Delete" button.  You will be asked to confirm the deletion
before the entry is removed from the list.  When you are finished, you
can press "Cancel" to close the Hot List if you don't want to slew the
telescope to any of the remaining Hot List objects.

**Set the Observation Date & Time**
To set the "current" date and time for the telescope, close the radio
telescope control panel, then select "File"->"Set Observation Date/Time,
ID..." from the observatory control panel menu.

**Close the Radio Telescope Control Panel**
To close the radio telescope control panel, select "File"->"Exit" from
the menu, or use any of the standard Windows features (System Menu or
"X" box) to close the window.  You will be reminded of any unsaved data
and given a chance to save it before the telescope closes.

**Access Receivers**
To access the main or "master" receiver, press the "Receiver" button on
the radio telescope control panel.  Once the master receiver window
appears, up to two additional channels or "slave" receivers may be
accessed by pressing the "Add Channel" button on the master receiver
panel.

**Receiver Controls**
The following controls are used to operate the receivers.  Note that
some of the controls are located only on the main or "master" receiver.
When receiving, the Horizontal Seconds, Add Channel, and Record controls
are disabled.  When recording, all controls except Receiver Mode, Align
Receivers and Volume are disabled.

    Receiver Frequency - This control sets the receiver frequency.  Each
    click on the appropriate arrowed button increases or decreases the
    frequency by the amount (MHz) determined by the Frequency Increment
    control.  The frequency can be set to values between 400.00 and
    1400.00 MHz.  The Receiver Frequency control appears on all
    receivers, and is disabled during recording.

    Frequency Increment - This control sets the increment for the
    Receiver Frequency control.  The increment can be varied between
    0.01 and 100 MHz by factors of ten.  The Receiver Frequency control
    appears on all receivers, and is disabled during recording.

    Vertical Gain - This control sets the amplification applied to the
    signal by the receiver.  This affects both the "height" of the
    signal on the display and the strength of the recorded signal.  The
    vertical gain can be varied between 0.125 and 8.0 (arbitrary units)
    by factors of two.  The Vertical Gain control appears on all
    receivers, and is disabled during recording.

    Horizontal Seconds - This control sets the time scale of the
    received signal.  In addition to establishing the horizontal scale
    of the display and the recorded signal, this control alters the
    "time constant" or effective integration time of the receiver. Thus,
    setting the horizontal seconds to a higher value will aid in the
    detection of faint signals by increasing the signal to noise ratio,
    while lower values give visibility to the pulse shapes and provide
    for more accurate measuring of pulse positions.  The horizontal
    seconds can be varied between 0.5 and 16.0 by factors of two.
    Setting this control affects all open receivers, thus the control
    appears only on the main receiver (although the readout appears on
    all receivers).  The Horizontal Seconds control is disabled during
    both receiving and recording.

    Add Channel - Pressing this button opens a secondary or "slave"
    receiver.  The frequency and vertical gain of secondary receivers
    can be set independently, all other parameters and controls are
    established by the main receiver.  A maximum of two secondary
    receivers can be added.  The "Add Channel" control is disabled
    during both receiving and recording.  Secondary receivers can be
    closed without closing the main receiver, but certain restrictions
    apply - see the Help topic "Close Receivers".

    Align Receivers - This button switches receiver alignment on ("yes")
    and off ("no").  When receiver alignment is off, all open receivers
    can be moved and positioned independently, anywhere on the screen.
    When alignment is on, the receivers position themselves in a stack
    below the main receiver and "follow" the main receiver whenever it
    is moved.  Secondary receivers cannot be moved independently when
    alignment is on.  This control is always enabled.

    Receiver Mode - This button is used to start and stop the receivers.
    When the mode is set to "Receive", all open receivers begin
    displaying the received signals.  Recording also begins if the
    Record Switch is "on", and sounds are heard if the computer is
    properly equipped and sound effects are enabled.  To stop
    receiving/recording, press the Receiver Mode button again to switch
    the mode to "Stop".  The receiving/recording/sound will then stop
    when the display reaches the end of the current panel.  (Also see
    the Help topic "Record Signals".)

    Record Switch - This button turns signal recording on and off.  This
    button is enabled only when the receiver mode is "Stop" (see above).
    See the Help topic "Record Signals" for more information.

    Volume Control - This control will appear on the main receiver only
    if the computer is properly equipped for sound reproduction and the
    sound features of this software have been enabled.  Use it to
    control the volume of the sound during receiving/recording.  See the
    Help Topic "Sounds" for more information.

**Receive Signals**
To receive pulsar signals, accurately point the telescope at the
position of the pulsar.  The telescope tracking should be on.  (If you
are using a meridian transit telescope, see the note below.)  Once the
telescope has stopped moving, access the receiver.  To begin receiving a
signal, press the "Mode" button.  To stop, press the "Mode" button
again; reception will stop at the end of the current display.  Note that
when signals are being received you can change the frequency and the
vertical gain individually for each active receiver.  When reception is
stopped you can, in addition, open new receiver channels, adjust the
time scale (horizontal seconds), and activate data recording.

You should adjust each receiver so that the pulses are clearly visible.
If the signals are buried in the noise, it may help to increase the
Horizontal Seconds; this will increase the signal to noise ratio.  Use
the Vertical Gain to adjust the pulse height; the pulses should be
clearly visible but should not reach or be clipped by the top of the
display.

NOTE: For a meridian transit telescope, you must pick an observation
time when the pulsar is just to the East of the telescope's meridian.
Accurately position the declination of the telescope, and begin
receiving.  As the pulsar enters the telescope beam, you will begin to
receive the pulses.  They will get stronger, then begin to weaken and
finally disappear as the Earth's motion carries the telescope beam past
the object.  You must do all your observations, including recording the
signal, during the short time that the pulsar is in the beam.

**Record Signals**
To record the signals received from a pulsar, first perform the steps
described under the Help Topic "Receive Signals".  When you have
adjusted all receivers to give clear, easily identifiable pulses, stop
receiving, press the "Record" button to switch recording on, and begin
receiving again.  Everything you see on the receiver screens will be
recorded, up to a maximum of 4 full screens (2 to 64 seconds, depending
on the Horizontal Seconds setting).  To stop recording, press the Mode
button to stop receiving (but you will never record more than the first
four screens).

Recorded signals can be measured in using the radio pulsar analysis tool,
and/or saved for later analysis.

**Sounds**
If your computer is properly equipped, and the sound features of the
software are enabled, you will hear the sounds of the pulses and the
background noise when signals are being received.  You can adjust the
sound volume by using the volume control on the main receiver.  The
sound will accurately reflect the period and signal strength of the
pulsar being observed.

The sound will, in general, be synchronized to the display on the main
receiver.  However, since sound and video on PCs are controlled by
completely separate and independent subsystems, it is possible for the
sound and video to get "out of synch".  This will probably be noticeable
only for pulsars with periods longer than about 1/2 second.  Stopping
and restarting the receiver may get the sound and video back in synch.

**Measure Received Signals**
Received signals can be measured directly on the receiver screen, once
reception has stopped.  Press and drag with the left mouse button to
create and move a vertical cursor on each receiver screen.  The position
of the cursor, given in seconds from the left edge of the display,
appears on the upper left of each screen.  If you press and drag with
the right mouse button, you will create and move a single "reference"
cursor that appears in the same position on all open receiver screens.
Its readout appears on the upper right of all screens.

You are limited by this method to measurements of only one screen (time
span) of data.  For longer time spans, more accurate measurements (with
zooming and panning), and measurement recording features, you should
record the receiver data and use the radio pulsar analysis tool.

**Save Received Signals**
Recorded signals are held in RAM.  In this state they can be passed
directly to the data analysis tool, but if you do not plan to do this
immediately, or need a "permanent" record for any other reason, you
should save this data to a file.  (Recorded signals in RAM are erased
the next time recording is started.)  When you finish recording a signal
you are given a chance to save them immediately, or transfer immediately
to the data analysis window.  If you do not save at this point, you can
save later by selecting "File"->"Save Recorded Data..." from the radio
telescope control menu, or "File"->"Save..." from the data analysis tool
menu.

When you elect to save the file, a form appears allowing you to enter or
select a name for the saved file.  You can use any name you like, the
program will suggest a name generated from your login information.  You
should always use the qualifier ".PLR" on these files to avoid
difficulties and confusion later.

**Close Receivers**
Receiver windows are closed via the System Menu (click on the small box
in the upper left corner of the receiver window, then select "Close") or
the "X" box in the upper right corner of the window.  Closing the master
receiver window closes all open receiver windows. Slave receivers may be
closed individually, but must be closed in the reverse order of their
creation (i.e., close the last-created receiver first).
