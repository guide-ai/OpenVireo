**"Tracking"**
The tracking button turns on the sidereal tracking that keeps the
telescope pointed at a particular spot on the sky as the earth turns.
When tracking is off, the stars will drift through the telescope field.
Tracking is off when a telescope is first accessed.  The other telescope
controls are enabled only when tracking is on.  The green indicator
light shows that tracking has been turned on.

**Slew Buttons ("N","S","E","W")**
The slew buttons are used to move the telescope to point to a particular
spot (direction) in the sky.  To move the telescope, press and hold the
appropriate button.  The telescope will slew at a speed determined by
the slew rate setting.  Release the button to stop the slew.  For long
slews, the button will stay down if you slide the cursor off of it
before releasing the mouse button.  In this case, stop the slew by
pressing and releasing the mouse button with the cursor over the active
button.

The slew buttons are primarily for making relatively small changes to
the position of the telescope to place objects in the active area of the
instrument being used.  To move the telescope to a different field or
object, use the "Slew" menu selection.

    -Keyboard Access-
    The arrow keys on the numeric keypad (keys "2","4","6","8") may be
    used to slew the telescope in place of the slew buttons. NumLock
    must be "On" and MouseKeys must be "Off".  (If you are using
    MouseKeys, configure your system so that MouseKeys are activated
    when the NumLock is "Off".)

**"Slew Rate"**
This slider switch sets the rate at which the telescope moves when a
slew button ("N","S","E","W") is pressed.  The rate doubles each time
the slider is moved one position to the right, up to a maximum
(relative) rate of 16.  Similarly, the slew rate is cut in half each
time the slider is moved one position to the left.

**"View"**
Each telescope has two views (or field sizes) available.  The finder
field is 2 1/2 degrees on a side, and is used for positioning particular
stars and other objects in the higher magnification telescope field
(indicated by the red square in the center of the finder view).  The
telescope field is 15 arc minutes on a side and shows stars and other
objects to a much fainter magnitude limit than the finder.  This view is
used to position the object for observations with the selected
instrument.

To change the view, move the slider to "Finder" or "Telescope".
Instruments can be accessed only when the telescope view is active.

The active area of the selected instrument is outlined in red on the
telescope view.  The photometer aperture is shown as a red circle, while
the spectrometer slit is indicated by a short pair of close parallel
lines.  For the CCD and IR cameras, a square shows the size of the
camera field.  (For some combinations of telescope and camera, the field
is larger than the 15 arc minute view.  This is indicated by red arrows
pointing outward at the edges of the view.)

**"Instrument"**
To access an instrument, set the "Instrument" slider to the appropriate
position and press the "Access" button.  The instrument(s) available for
your use will depend on the exercise you are doing.  The "Access" button
is enabled only when the view is set to "Telescope".

**Slider Switches**
Several controls on the telescope and instrument control panels use
slider switches to enable the user to select one out of a number of
choices.  To make a selection with a slider switch, either:

    A)  Place the cursor over the slider and press, hold and drag the
        slider to the desired position, using the left mouse button,
        or...

    B)  Place the cursor over the desired position on the slider panel
        and click the left mouse button, or...

    C)  Keyboard Access: Use the arrow keys to move the slider when the
        desired slider control has been selected.  (The selected control
        is outlined with a dotted line.  Use Tab & Shift-Tab to change
        the selection.)

**"Slew"**
The "Slew" menu selection on the telescope control panel activates a
dialog that allows the pointing coordinates for the telescope to be
entered directly.  This is generally faster than using the slew buttons
to move the telescope from object to object, unless the objects are very
near each other in the sky.  Some "fine tuning" with the slew buttons
may be necessary to center the object in the spectrometer slit,
photometer aperture or camera field after setting the coordinates.

**Site and Telescope Parameters**
Select "File"->"Site Information..." from the telescope control panel
menu to obtain information on the current observing site and telescope.
Parameters listed include the site latitude, longitude, height above sea
level and time zone.  The telescope aperture and focal length are also
shown.
