**Log In**
To Log In to the Virtual Educational Observatory (VIREO):

    1)  Select "File"->"Log In..." from the observatory control panel
        menu.  The login dialog will appear.

    2)  Enter the appropriate name(s) in the fields provided.

    3)  The laboratory table number may already be set.  If it is not,
        or is incorrect, enter the correct table number.

    4)  Select "OK".  The selections under "File" that control the
        exercise will be activated, while the "Log In..." selection will
        be deactivated.  (You cannot return to the login dialog after
        you have completed it.)

The information entered on the login dialog is used to generate names
for data files, and will appear on data and printouts that are produced
by the exercise. If you have entered no information, a box will warn you
of this when you press "OK".  If you select "Cancel" on the warning box
you will return to the login dialog.  If you select "OK" on the warning
box, the program will continue.  In this case, generated data and files
will be identified as "NoLog".

Immediately after logging in you should select the exercise that you
will be doing.

**Select Exercise**
After you have successfully logged in, select "File"->"Run Exercise"
from the observatory control panel menu and pick the exercise from the
list.  The title screen for the selected exercise will be displayed for
five seconds, followed by the observatory control panel.  The exercise
you select will determine which of the numerous features and
capabilities of VIREO will be available to you.  (All features of VIREO
are available for the exercise "The Quest for Object X", and in the
non-specific Virtual Observatory mode.)

WARNING: You cannot change the exercise selection once you have made it.
If you make an error in the selection you must exit the program and
start over!

**Set Date & Time**
Some exercises performed with VIREO contain time-dependent elements.
When you start the exercise "The Quest for Object X", a small box will
appear for you to enter the date, time, and identification of the
unknown you have been assigned.  Be sure to set these to values provided
in your exercise material, or assigned directly by your instructor.
After you have entered the information and pressed the "OK" button,
there may be a slight pause as the software adjusts the data for the
exercise.

You may also be required to change the date/time at later points in some
exercises.  To do this, close the telescope control panel (optical
and/or radio) and select "File"->"Set Observation Date/Time, ID..." from
the observatory control panel menu.  (This menu selection is not
available for exercises that do not involve time-dependent elements. For
the "Radio Astronomy of Pulsars" exercise, and in the non-specific
Virtual Observatory mode, the menu selection is available, but you are
not required to set the date/time initially.)

**Select Telescope**
To access a telescope, select "Telescopes" from the observatory control
panel menu. Depending on the exercise, you will have access to one or
more optical telescopes and/or a radio telescope.  Make your selection
from the menu. Some of the optical telescope choices may be grayed,
which indicates their use is restricted and you must apply for time.

When you select a telescope, its name or identification will appear at
the bottom of the observatory control panel.  For optical telescopes,
the inside of the closed dome will appear in the display area.  For the
radio telescope, a picture or other graphic will appear.

**Open Dome**
After selecting an optical telescope, you must open the dome.  Press the
top half of the Open/Close switch.  The dome will begin to open and
various indicators will light to indicate the dome state.  To close the
dome, after it is open or has begun to open, press the bottom half of
the switch.

You must open the dome before you can access the telescope control
panel, and in general you must close it before you can change
telescopes.  Dome operation is not required for the radio telescope.

The initial image that appears in the dome opening shows naked-eye stars
directly to the south of the observatory location (north, for southern
hemisphere observatories) at the current date and time.  (These stars
may not actually be visible, because the software does not make a
day/night determination.)  When you close the telescope control panel
and return to the dome view, the image will show naked-eye stars in the
general direction that the telescope was pointing.  To identify the
stars, right-click directly on a star image.  A box will appear giving
the right ascension, declination and magnitude.  You can then use a star
chart to identify the stars and constellations.

**Access Telescope Control**
Once the dome is open (or when the radio telescope has been selected),
press the appropriate button on the panel labeled "Telescope Control
Panel" to access the telescope control.  The control panel will appear
after a few seconds.  (There may be a slight delay as the star field
image is being generated.)  In most cases the first thing you will want
to do is to turn on the tracking by pressing the appropriate button.

When the telescope control panel is open, the image on the observatory
control panel will go black.  Features accessed via the observatory
control panel menu are still available.

**Request Telescope Time**
Access to the larger telescopes may be restricted.  You may request time
on telescopes that are not currently available to you (the selections
are grayed).  To request time, select "Telescope"->"Optical"-> "Request
Time"->(telescope selection) from the observatory control panel menu.

If your request is granted:
    You will be informed of the number of uses you have been given (the
    number of spectra, photometric obsevations, CCD images, etc. you can
    take with the restricted telescope).  You can then access the
    telescope in the normal manner, but a tally will be kept of your
    uses, and you will be informed when you have "used up" your
    allotment.  You may then request more time if you wish.

If your request is not granted:
    You will be informed that you may reapply after a certain number of
    minutes.  Until that time has passed you will not be able to request
    time on that particular telescope.  When the time has elapsed a
    message will appear informing you that you may reapply.  You may do
    anything else you wish, including requesting and/or using other
    telescopes, analyzing data, etc. while waiting to reapply.

**Change Optical Telescope**
To change optical telescopes, select "Telescopes"->"Optical" from the
observatory control panel menu, and then make your selection from the
list that appears.  If a particular selection is grayed, you must
request and be awarded time on that particular telescope.

When you have selected a telescope to access, the dome on the current
telescope will close, and there will be a pause while the system
attempts to access the telescope you have selected.  If the selected
telescope is available, you will be given control.  Note that you must
again open the dome and turn on the tracking before you can proceed to
take spectra with the new telescope.  If the telescope you select is
restricted and you have been granted a limited number of uses, you will
be informed of your remaining uses before the telescope is accessed.

**HR Diagram Analysis Tool**

**Astrometry Module**
The astrometry module uses images taken with an optical telescope and
the CCD and/or infrared (IR) camera.  You can use this module to display
CCD & IR images, display/print the (FITS) image headers, search the
images for asteroids, and measure the positions of asteroids on the
images.  To access the astrometry module, select "Tools"->"Astrometry
Module" from the observatory control panel menu.

NOTE: This tool is not available for exercises that do not require it.

**Image Processing (DS9)**
A full-featured image processing program, "SAOImage DS9" is available
for use with VIREO.  To access, select "Tools"->"Image Processing (DS9)"
from the observatory control panel menu.  You can use DS9 to view images
you have taken with the CDD and IR cameras, and for many other image
processing tasks.  DS9 is a large program with many fuctions and
options.  You will be given instructions on its use by your instructor
or in your exercise materials if you are expected to employ it.

If you have taken images with one of the VIREO cameras and access DS9 to
view them, the program will point to the correct directory to load your
images.  However, you should be aware that its default file extension
for loading files is ".FITS", while VIREO-generated image files carry
the extension ".FIT".  Enter "*.FIT" in the file name field on the file
open display to see your image files.

NOTE: This tool is not available for exercises that do not require it.

**Results Editors**
Results editors enable you to work with results and other data that you
have generated using the telescopes, instruments, and other tools.  With
the results editors you can:

    1)  View and perform some editing operations on data that you have
        generated.
  
    2)  Print the results for inclusion with your exercise report or for
        your own use.

    3)  Save the data in a form that can be reloaded at a later date.
        This allows you to interrupt an exercise and continue later from
        where you left off, or generate an assigned subset of data for a
        larger group project.

    4)  Load previously saved data.

    5)  Save the data in an alternate format (.CSV) that can be read
        into a spreadsheet program for further analysis or graphing.

To access a results editor, select "Tools"->"Results Editors"->(type of
results)->"Display/Print/Save..." from the observatory control panel
menu.  The choices of available editor(s) will depend on the exercise
you are doing.

**Load Saved Data**
You can load a file of data that was previously created and saved using
this program and one of the results editors.  This feature allows you to
interrupt your exercise and continue at a later time from where you left
off, and to accumulate data over several sessions as part of a project.
You can delay computing and/or plotting the final results until all
stars have been measured.

To load saved data, select "Tools"->"Results Editors"->(type of results)
->"Load Saved Data..." from the observatory control panel menu. The
choices available will depend on the exercise you are doing.

When loading data you should be aware of the following:

    1)  When a file is loaded, it replaces any data currently present.
        You will be warned to save first if you attempt to load over
        existing data.

    2)  Any data generated after a file load is added to the loaded data
        in memory.  You will have to save it to keep the complete set.
 
    4)  The format of the file to be loaded must be same as written.  In
        other words, you must select the same results editor to load the
        data as was used to save it.

**Spreadsheet**
Results analysis activities, such as computations, formatting of results
for printing and preparation of charts and graphs may be facilitated by
making use of a spreadsheet program.  If a spreadsheet program is
available for your use, the "Spreadsheet" selection under "Tools" on the
observatory control panel menu will be dark black.  (If this entry is
grey, no spreadsheet program is available.)  The following outlines the
steps to follow in using a spreadsheet:

    1)  Take and record necessary measurements for all required stars or
        other objects.

    2)  Using a results editor, save your data in spreadsheet format
        (.CSV).  You should make a note of the file name.

    3)  Select "Tools"->"Spreadsheet" from the observatory control panel
        menu to access the spreadsheet program.

    4)  Load your data file into the spreadsheet (choose file type
        "Text" - qualifier .CSV).  In most cases, the spreadsheet should
        start with your data already loaded, or at least with the
        correct folder accessed. Your instructor will assist you with
        this and other spreadsheet activities.

    5)  Use the spreadsheet to perform calculations, format and print
        data, prepare and print charts and graphs, etc. as directed by
        your instructor.

    6)  If so instructed, write the completed spreadsheet back out as a
        comma delimited file (in Excel, choose "File"->"Save As..." type
        CSV).

    7)  Exit the spreadsheet (in Excel, choose "File"->"Exit").

**End Exercise**
Select "File"->"Exit Observatory" from the observatory control panel
menu to end the exercise.  If you have saved any spectra, CCD images,
pulsar data or results lists they may remain in the machine so that you
can restart the program and access them at a later date.  However, your
installation may not support this - ask your instructor to be sure.  To
be safe, it is best to print all results before closing the program.

**Keyboard Access**
Keyboard (mouse-free) access is provided to the facilities of VIREO (see
NOTE below).  The general features of keyboard access are as follows:

    -Menus-
    All menu selections have assigned hot keys.  To access menu
    selections from the keyboard, press the "Alt" key and the hot key
    selections will appear as underlined characters in the menu.  To
    make a slection, press the appropriate hot key.  Alternatively,
    after the "Alt" key has been pressed, use the arrow keys to move
    between menu slections and the "Enter" key to select.
 
    -Controls-
    You can move between the controls (buttons, switches, sliders, text
    boxes, etc.) on any form by using the "Tab" and "Shift-Tab" keys.
    (The arrow keys may sometimes be used for this, but will not work
    with all controls.)  The currently selected control is indicated by
    a dotted outline, or by a color change in a text box.  Once
    selected, buttons may be pressed using either the Space Bar or the
    "Enter" key.  Switches and sliders are usually operated with the
    arrow keys.

    -Cursors-
    On forms which use a moving cursor for identification and/or
    measurement purposes, you can move the cursor using the arrow keys
    on the numeric keypad (keys "2","4","6","8").  These keys may also
    be used to slew the telescope.  You must have NumLock "On" and
    MouseKeys "Off" to use this feature.  (See "MouseKeys" below.)

    -MouseKeys-
    If you are using MouseKeys, configure your system so that MouseKeys
    are active when the NumLock is "Off", to avoid a conflict with the
    use of the numeric keypad arrow keys (above).  You should not need
    to use MouseKeys for keyboard accsss of the basic features of VIREO
    (but see NOTE below).

NOTE: Complete (we hope) keyboard access is provided in the main control
and optical observatory facilities of VIREO.  However, in this version
(1.0), facilities that were lifted from earlier software packages
(primarily the radio observatory and asteroid astrometry features) are
not totally keyboard accessible, although some improvements have been
made.  If you wish to use all of the features of these facilities from
the keyboard, you will have to use MouseKeys.
