**Find Pulsar In List**
Both B1950 and J2000 pulsar names are maintained in the list (the more
recently discoverd pulsars may not have a B1950 name).  To find a
pulsar by name:

    o  Select "File"->"Find Pulsar..." from the Pulsar Parameters Menu.
    o  Enter the pulsar name in the box.
    o  Select the name type.
    o  Press "OK".

You can use a partial name, as long as it starts from the beginning.
(Example: search on "045", then use "Next" & "Prev" buttons to find the
one you want.)  Since pulsar names are created from their positions, you
can use this technique to find a pulsar whose Right Ascension you know.
All searches start at the beginning of the list, not the current
record.

**Change Date - Compute Period**
The current period is obtained from the current observation date, the
PDot parameter, and the PEpoch parameter.  To compute the period at a
different date:

    o  Edit the Julian Date value in the field labeled "Date (MJD)".
    o  Press the "Update" button to recompute the period.
    o  To return to the current observation date, press "Reset".

Alternatively, you can change the current observation date, in normal
date format. To do this:

    o  Select "File"->"Date/Time..." from the Telescope Control Panel
       Menu.  (You do not have to close the Pulsar Parameters display to
       do this.  Just move it out of the way or minimize it.)
    o  Enter the desired date/time in the appropriate boxes.
    o  Press "Ok".
    o  Return to the Pulsar Parameters display.  You may want to press
       "Reset" to assure that the new date is used.  (This should happen
       automatically when the display is re-accessed.

**Change Electron Density - Compute Distance**
The derived distance is computed from the Dispersion parameter and the
Electron Density.  To see the effect of changes in the Electron Density
on the computed distance:

    o  Edit the Electron Density value in the labeled field.
    o  Press the "Update" button to recompute the distance.

**Return to Current Values**
The "Reset" button will return the display to the current observation
date/time and the default electron density of 0.03.  Since the clock is
always running, there will always be a small change in the JD when you
do this, but the magnitudes of the PDot values are so small that this
will usually have no noticeable effect on the period.

**Select Pulsars for Study**
In selecting pulsars for students to observe, instructors should pick
objects from the list that have recorded flux densities at all three
frequencies (400, 600 & 1400 MHz).  Also be aware that it is difficult
to detect pulses with the default instrument and gain factors if the
flux density is less than 10.0.  To work with the fainter pulsars,
increase the Telescope Aperature and/or the Base Gain Factor in the
Options.
