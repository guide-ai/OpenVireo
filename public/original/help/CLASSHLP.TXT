**Load Spectrum**
To load a spectrum for classification:

    1)  Select "File"->"Unknown Spectra" from the classification window
        menu.

    2)  Make a selection from the sub-menu that appears:

        "Saved Spectra (*.CSP...)"
            Select from a list of spectra taken and saved for
            classification as part of this exercise.  To select,
            double-click on the file name or move the cursor to the file
            name and press "Open".
  
        "Program List"
            Select from a list of program stars for this execise.  You
            will normally begin with the first star on the list.  To
            select a star on the list, double-click on the star ID or
            move the cursor to the desired star and select "File"->
            "Open Selection" from the menu.

        "Next On List"
            Loads the next spectrum on the program list without
            displaying the list.  ("Program List" must have been
            selected previously.)

The selected spectrum is loaded and displayed in the center of the
spectrum display area.

**Load Standard Spectra**
To load standard spectra for comparison:

    1)  Select "File"->"Atlas of Standard Spectra..." from the
        classification window menu.

    2)  Select an atlas from the list that appears.  You will normally
        begin with the Main Sequence to establish a temperature class
        before considering luminosity.  To select, double-click on the
        atlas description or move the cursor to the desired atlas and
        select "File"->"Open Selection" from the menu.

    3)  The list of standards in the atlas will appear in the teal
        "Standards" display on the right of the classification window.
        Note that the highlighted spectrum is displayed above the
        unknown in the display area, while the next atlas entry is
        displayed below the unknown.

    4)  Use the arrow keys or click on entries in the standards list to
        peruse the atlas. Note the progressive changes to the spectra as
        the stellar temperature changes.  Look for a spectrum in the
        atlas that most closely matches the unknown.   You may have to
        check luminosity (load another atlas) to get the closest match.

**Measure/Mark Spectral Features**
To measure the wavelength and intensity of a spectral feature:

    With the cursor over the spectrum display, press and hold the left
    mouse button - the cursor changes from an arrow to a crosshair.  As
    you move the cursor within the spectrum display area, the wavelength
    and relative intensity reading at the cursor are displayed at the
    bottom of the window.  When you have positioned the cursor at the
    point you wish to measure, release the mouse button. The readings
    will remain unchanged until the left mouse button is pressed again.

        NOTE:  If the spectrum is displayed as an intensity trace (see
        Help topic "Configure Display"), it is the vertical postion (Y
        coordinate) of the cursor that determines the intensity
        measurement.  If the display is in "Photo" mode, the intensity
        "under" the cursor (at the horizontal or X coordinate) is
        displayed.

    When you release the mouse button, the cursor returns to its normal
    shape, and the measured position is marked on the screen by a
    vertical red line spanning the unknown and standard spectra at the
    wavelength position.  A small horizontal line indicates the
    intensity position.  You can also mark a line by double-clicking its
    entry in the spectral line table.  You can "fine tune" the position
    of the lines by using the arrow keys on the numeric keypad (see
    below).

    -Keyboard Access-
    The arrow keys on the numeric keypad (keys "2","4","6","8") may be
    use to position the measuring lines in place of the mouse. NumLock
    must be "On" and MouseKeys must be "Off".  (If you are using
    MouseKeys, configure your system so that MouseKeys are activated
    when the NumLock is "Off".)  If the measuring lines are not visible,
    press the right arrow key ("6"), and the lines will appear from the
    left of the display.

**Controls**
The following controls are located on the right-hand side of the
classification window:

    Zoom In (up arrowhead):
        Magnifies the displayed spectra by a factor of 2, up to a
        maximum of 16 times the original display.  If a feature is
        marked, the zoom attempts to center it in the magnified display.
        If no line is marked, the wavelength of the center of the
        display remains the same.

    Zoom Out (down arrowhead):
        Decreases the magnification of the displayed spectra by a factor
        of 2, down to the original magnification (1X).  If a line is
        marked, the zoom attempts to keep it centered in the display.

    Pan Arrows (left & right arrowheads):
        Use the left and right arrow controls to scan across magnified
        spectra.

    "Reset" button:
        Returns the display to the original (1X) images.

**Spectral Line Table & Help**
The spectral line table assists in the identification of spectral
features by wavelength, and includes a help facility that provides
information on the significance and use of each tabulated line in the
classification of stellar spectra.

To load the spectral line table, select "File"->"Spectral Line Table"
from the classification menu.  Once the line table appears, you can move
it to a convenient place on the screen.  (Place the cursor in the title
bar at the top.  Press and hold the left mouse button, and drag the
window.)

Features of the spectral line table:

    -Cursor Tracking-
        When the left mouse button is depressed with the cursor anywhere
        within the spectrum display, the entry having a wavelength
        nearest to the cursor position is highlighted on the spectral
        line table. The table will scroll to track the cursor when the
        mouse is moved with the left button depressed.

            CAUTION: Judgement must be used in accepting line
            identifications from the table, since wavelength is the only
            criteria - temperature, luminosity, etc. are not taken into
            account.   For example, iron lines (Fe) do not appear in
            early-type stars, helium lines (He) do not appear in
            late-type stars, etc.

    -Marking Line Position-
        Clicking the left mouse button on an entry in the spectral line
        table highlights the entry, and the position of the line is
        marked on the spectrum display.

    -Help-
        When the left mouse button is double-clicked on an entry in the
        spectral line table, the entry is highlighted and the line
        marked as above, and a Help window will appear with information
        about the selected line.  Once opened, the Help window will
        continue to "track" the table selections (without
        double-clicking) until the Help window is closed.

**Configure Display**
Options affecting the appearance and operation of the classification
window are available through the "File"->"Display" menu selection.

    "File"->"Display"
        Sets the format of the spectrum display.  The following options
        are available:

        "Show Difference (Std - Unkn)"
            When this option is selected, the lower standard spectrum is
            replaced by a display showing the point by point difference
            between the upper standard spectrum and the unknown.  This
            is a very useful aid to spectral classification

        "Intensity Trace"
            Displays spectra as plots of relative intensity vs
            wavelength (default).

        "Grayscale "Photo""
            Converts relative intensities to grayscale levels to mimic a
            (positive) photographic image.

        "Comb. (Photo & Trace)"
            Displays the unknown in both modes.  One standard is
            displayed as a "Photo".

        "Reverse Grayscale Displays"
            Reverses the conversion of intensities to grayscale levels
            to mimic a negative photographic image.


**Measure Equivalent Widths**
The "equivalent width" of a spectral line is a computed quantity that
accurately measures the strength of the line.  If you are attempting to
classify a stellar spectrum very accurately, and are uncertain as to the
relative strengths of important features, the equivalent width measure
can help you.  To measure the equivalent width of a spectral feature:

    1)  Zoom the display to a magnification that allows the "shoulders"
        of the line to be clearly identified and marked.

    2)  Select "Line Equivalent Width..." from the classification menu.
        A box will appear with instructions for completing the
        equivalent width calculation process (a summary of steps 3-5
        below).

    3)  Mark the approxmate center of the line on the spectrum display
        by using the left mouse button on either the display itself or
        on the line entry in the table.

    4)  Mark the wavelength and intensity of both shoulders of the line
        by clicking the right mouse button with the cursor at each
        shoulder in turn.  The vertical position of the cursor should
        indicate the continuum level on each side of the line. If you
        have done this correctly the extents of the line will be
        delineated by a blue box, with the red line position marker
        passing somewhere near its center.  If you wish to change a
        position, simply move the cursor to the new position and click
        the right button again.  You may also reposition the line center
        marker by clicking the left button.

    5)  When the feature is correctly delineated, press "OK" on the
        instruction box.  The computed equivalent width, as well as
        wavelengths and intensities at the marked line center and
        shoulders will be displayed.

**Save, Review, Analyze Results**
When you have classified a spectrum to your satisfaction, select
"Classification Results" from the menu and record your classification in
the box labeled "Sp. Type" on the form that appears.  You may also
record any uncertainties or unusual features of the spectrum in the
"Remarks" box.  When you press "OK" the information you entered is
placed on a list, but is not saved to a file.  To review, print, and/or
save your results to a file, select "Tools"->"Results Editors"->
"Observational Results"->"Display/Print/Save" on the observatory
control panel menu.

Related Topics:
    "Results Editors" on the observatory control panel "Help" menu.

**Set Wavelength Range**
You can change the range of wavelengths visible in the classification
window, within the maximum range of 3500 to 7400 Angstroms.  To change
the wavelength range, select "File"->"Display"->"Spectral Range" from
the classification control panel menu.  Use the sliders to change the
minimum and/or maximum values.  The "Default" button returns the values
to the optimal range for spectral classification.  Note that if you
extend the range, you may exceed the limits of the spectrum you are
classifying.

**Close Classification Window**
To close the classification window and return to the observatory control
panel, select "File"->"Exit Classify Spectra" on the menu, or press the
"X" box in the upper right corner.
